# VISUAL DIRECTION EXPLORATION v1.1

Status: DRAFT - PHASE 1A AMENDED
Date: 2026-09-04
Truth level: PROVISIONAL

## Purpose

Phase 1A explores four materially different candidate art directions for the CAPICAPI base character before any 3D master is modeled.

The goal is not to choose the cutest capybara or the best render. The goal is to identify proprietary visual DNA that survives role, costume, material, and series changes while remaining compatible with Bambu Lab A1 FDM production.

## Phase 1A Boundary

Do not:

- model Capi v0
- freeze a consumer character
- select final proportions
- select filament colors
- create Series 01
- approve a permanent production master
- treat generated candidate images as final reference sheets

Do:

- define candidate visual directions
- generate/evaluate reference images later
- compare directions using identical views
- test whether visual DNA survives expression and role stress tests
- require human approval before Phase 1B

## Four Candidate Directions

These are exploration lanes, not final choices. None is approved.

### Direction A - Grounded Loaf Collectible

Premise: low, calm, compact, strongly capybara-derived, high animal recognition, minimal mascot/humanization.

Distinctive questions:

- Can a low animal-forward form become proprietary without becoming generic?
- Does the muzzle/ear/eye language carry enough identity with no costume?
- Can future accessories attach without hiding the base character?

Risks:

- may become too generic if silhouette is not specific
- low body may limit costume readability
- side view must avoid becoming an anonymous oval

### Direction B - Designer Toy Mascot

Premise: more iconic collectible proportions, stronger head/body separation, broader expression and costume capability while retaining clear capybara anatomy.

Distinctive questions:

- Can CAPICAPI become more character-forward without losing capybara identity?
- Are expressions and costumes more readable than Direction A?
- Does the body avoid drifting into teddy bear, chibi, or generic mascot territory?

Risks:

- may over-humanize the base
- may invite fragile limbs or accessories
- head/body separation must remain FDM-friendly

### Direction C - Expressive Stylized / Anime-Adjacent

Premise: a more expressive character-oriented interpretation suitable for roles such as Biker, Samurai, Wizard, Gamer, or equivalent, while remaining recognizably CAPICAPI and FDM-compatible.

`Anime-adjacent` means expressive graphic language, readable emotion, dynamic character energy, and bold simplified shape design. It does not mean generic anime eyes, human anatomy, thin human limbs, or a capybara merely wearing a human costume.

Distinctive questions:

- Can CAPICAPI support stronger emotion and role energy without losing capybara anatomy?
- Can the eye and face language become expressive while staying simple enough for FDM?
- Can this direction scale into character roles without becoming cosplay-first?

Risks:

- may drift into generic anime mascot language
- may over-prioritize expression over base silhouette
- may add detail density that fails FDM or color efficiency

### Direction D - Sculptural Art Toy

Premise: stronger plane/silhouette design, reduced surface noise, premium sculptural character, highly deliberate geometry.

Distinctive questions:

- Can a more sculptural form feel premium and proprietary?
- Does simplified plane language improve FDM readability?
- Can role variants be communicated through relief, silhouette zones, and controlled accessories rather than many colors?

Risks:

- may feel less emotionally accessible
- expression range may be limited
- must avoid becoming abstract enough to lose capybara identity

## Base Character Round

Before generating costume, role, prop, or expression concepts, every direction must complete a base character round.

All four base candidates must use:

- identical neutral background
- identical visual scale
- equivalent camera framing
- no costume
- no accessories
- no props
- no environmental scene
- no special materials
- neutral expression

Required views:

- front
- front 3/4
- side
- back
- silhouette

Purpose: compare character DNA rather than render quality, costume appeal, lighting tricks, material tricks, or environmental styling.

Failure condition: if a direction is not recognizable and distinctive in the base character round, it does not advance to expression or role stress testing without revision.

## Expression Test

Only surviving directions from the base character round receive expression testing.

Expression set:

- neutral
- curious
- sleepy
- proud
- surprised

Rules:

- keep base anatomy consistent across expressions
- do not change the head/body relationship to force emotion
- do not introduce generic anime eyes, human face anatomy, or unprintable micro-detail
- expression must remain readable at collectible scale

## Role Stress Test

Only after base character and expression review should each surviving direction receive role stress tests.

Required role briefs:

- Biker
- Astronaut
- Doctor

Use the same role briefs across every direction. The role test exists to evaluate whether visual DNA survives costume and accessory changes, not to pick a final character or Series 01 concept.

Role test rules:

- preserve the base silhouette and facial identity
- keep accessories fused or clearly fusible later
- avoid props that require separate parts
- avoid costumes that hide the muzzle, ears, body language, or stance
- do not select final colors or filament SKUs

## Evaluation Scorecard

Score each direction from 1 to 5 for each criterion. Scores are advisory only; human judgment remains authoritative.

| Criterion | Score | Notes |
|---|---:|---|

| proprietary/distinctive silhouette | TBD | Is the outline ownable and repeatable? |
| emotional appeal | TBD | Does it create attachment without depending on costume? |
| recognizability without costume | TBD | Does the base character remain CAPICAPI when naked/base? |
| expression range | TBD | Can it support neutral, curious, sleepy, proud, and surprised? |
| costume flexibility | TBD | Can roles attach without hiding the base? |
| Series scalability | TBD | Can many future variants share this DNA? |
| FDM feasibility | TBD | Does the form look modellable and printable on the A1? |
| support-free potential | TBD | Does the silhouette avoid obvious support traps? |
| geometry-detail efficiency | TBD | Does shape do the work without excessive micro-detail? |
| color efficiency | TBD | Can it read with limited automatic colors? |

Do not automatically select the highest score. Use the scorecard to expose tradeoffs, risks, and revision needs for human review.

## Visual Reference Policy

External references are inspiration and analysis inputs only. They are not source geometry, not trace targets, not copied design systems, and not a substitute for CAPICAPI-owned master references.

Every external reference must be categorized by purpose:

- `proportion` - used to discuss mass relationships, stance, scale, or body rhythm
- `face_language` - used to discuss eye, muzzle, brow, mouth, or expression systems
- `silhouette` - used to discuss outline strength, recognizability, or pose clarity
- `surface_treatment` - used to discuss planes, relief, texture restraint, or material simplification
- `accessory_integration` - used to discuss how role elements attach to a base character
- `negative_do_not_copy` - used to mark drift, genericness, legal/IP risk, or style traps

Reference handling rules:

- record why the reference is included
- never use one reference as the complete answer
- combine analysis into original CAPICAPI decisions
- do not copy protected characters, distinctive costumes, logos, markings, or signature proportions
- do not let external style overwhelm capybara recognition
- treat `negative_do_not_copy` examples as boundaries, not targets

After the CAPICAPI Master is approved, the CAPICAPI Master becomes the dominant visual authority for future variants. External references may still inform accessories, materials, or role language, but they must not override the approved master.

## Base Character Generation Briefs

Use these prompts only to generate the Base Character Round. Do not generate costume, expression, role, color, or final 3D geometry from these prompts.

Shared generation constraints for all four prompts:

```text
Create a clean character design exploration sheet for one CAPICAPI base character direction. Use an identical plain neutral background, identical visual scale across views, equivalent camera framing, no costume, no accessories, no props, no environmental scene, no special materials, and a neutral expression. Show exactly five views: front, front 3/4, side, back, and a solid black silhouette view. The character must be a stylized capybara-derived collectible figure, original and proprietary, suitable for later FDM 3D printing on a Bambu Lab A1. Keep forms simple, sturdy, rounded or deliberately planar as requested, with no thin fragile details, no separate parts, no unprintable micro-detail, no selected filament colors, and no final numeric proportions. This is an exploratory visual candidate, not a final master.
```

## Decision Workflow

1. Human reviews these four Base Character Round prompts.
2. Generate Direction A base sheet only after prompt approval.
3. Generate Direction B base sheet using the same constraints.
4. Generate Direction C base sheet using the same constraints.
5. Generate Direction D base sheet using the same constraints.
6. Normalize all sheets for identical background, scale, framing, and view order.
7. Score the base characters with the evaluation scorecard.
8. Human chooses which directions survive, require revision, or are rejected.
9. Only surviving directions receive expression tests.
10. Only surviving expression-tested directions receive role stress tests.
11. Human chooses: approve one for Phase 1B, revise, combine specific traits, request more exploration, or reject all.
12. Only after approval, enter Phase 1B provisional visual master/reference pack.
