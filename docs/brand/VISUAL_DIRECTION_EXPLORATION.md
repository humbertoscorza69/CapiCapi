## Purpose

Phase 1A explores the physical DNA of the CAPICAPI base character before any 3D master is modeled. Round 0 explored four art-direction lanes. The owner reviewed its twelve candidates and requested a new morphological experiment without selecting a winner or authorizing downstream development.

The goal is not to choose the cutest capybara or the best render. The goal is to identify proprietary visual DNA that survives role, costume, material, and series changes while remaining compatible with Bambu Lab A1 FDM production.

## Four Candidate Directions - Round 0 Historical Lanes

These are exploration lanes, not final choices. None is approved.

### Direction A - Grounded Loaf Collectible

Premise: low, calm, compact, strongly capybara-derived, high animal recognition, minimal mascot/humanization.

Distinctive questions:

- Can a low animal-forward form become proprietary without becoming generic?
- Does the muzzle/ear/eye language carry enough identity with no costume?
- Can future accessories attach without hiding the base character?

Risks:

- may become too generic if silhouette is not specific
- low body may limit costume readability
- side view must avoid becoming an anonymous oval

### Direction B - Designer Toy Mascot

Premise: more iconic collectible proportions, stronger head/body separation, broader expression and costume capability while retaining clear capybara anatomy.

Distinctive questions:

- Can CAPICAPI become more character-forward without losing capybara identity?
- Are expressions and costumes more readable than Direction A?
- Does the body avoid drifting into teddy bear, chibi, or generic mascot territory?

Risks:

- may over-humanize the base
- may invite fragile limbs or accessories
- head/body separation must remain FDM-friendly

### Direction C - Expressive Stylized Character / Game-Character Language

Premise: a more expressive character-oriented capybara interpretation suitable for roles such as Biker, Samurai, Wizard, Gamer, or equivalent, while remaining recognizably CAPICAPI and FDM-compatible.

This direction may carry anime-adjacent energy through graphic shape rhythm, expression potential, and stronger character hierarchy. It must not use anime eye construction, sparkling oversized eyes, human facial structure, human body anatomy, hair/anime hairstyle language, or a capybara merely wearing a human costume.

Distinctive questions:

- Can CAPICAPI support stronger emotion and role energy without losing capybara anatomy?
- Can the eye and face language become expressive while staying simple enough for FDM?
- Can this direction scale into character roles without becoming cosplay-first?

Risks:

- may drift into generic anime mascot or game mascot language
- may over-prioritize expression over base silhouette
- may add detail density that fails FDM or color efficiency

### Direction D - Sculptural Art Toy

Premise: stronger plane/silhouette design, reduced surface noise, premium sculptural character, highly deliberate geometry.

Distinctive questions:

- Can a more sculptural form feel premium and proprietary?
- Does simplified plane language improve FDM readability?
- Can role variants be communicated through relief, silhouette zones, and controlled accessories rather than many colors?

Risks:

- may feel less emotionally accessible
- expression range may be limited
- must avoid becoming abstract enough to lose capybara identity

## Round 1 - Signature Morphological Hook Experiment

### Research Question

With color, costume, accessories, props, branding, role, and special expression removed, can a simple structural rule make the neutral form memorable as a potential CAPICAPI character while it still immediately reads as capybara-derived?

Three requirements apply together: DISTINCTIVE MORPHOLOGY + CAPYBARA RECOGNITION + ACCESSORY COMPATIBILITY. CAPICAPI is a repeatable character platform for future Series and roles, not just a standalone sculpture. A strange form that loses capybara recognition fails one requirement; a pleasant generic capybara leaves another unresolved; a memorable naked form with no coherent dressing architecture fails the third. "Ownable" here is a design hypothesis about recognizable, repeatable form, not a claim of legal exclusivity or proven market recognition.

This round varies character architecture, proportion logic, silhouette, and mass distribution. It does not compare rendering styles, faceting, eye decoration, or progressively larger chibi heads. Each family must be describable as a construction rule without relying on a costume, color, or facial expression.

### Proposed Scope

- Ten morphological families, M01-M10, with two independent hero samples each: 20 images total if approved.
- Candidate IDs: R1-M01-S1 / R1-M01-S2 through R1-M10-S1 / R1-M10-S2. IDs are metadata, never text in the image.
- Both samples use the exact same shared prompt plus the exact same family prompt. They are independent generations, not edits of each other or unrecorded prompt revisions.
- Two samples provide a limited variance check, not statistical proof. Screen both samples within each family before comparing structural hypotheses across families.
- M01-M09 isolate different dominant structural decisions; M10 explicitly tests a combination. Avoid adding secondary exaggerations that collapse the isolated hypotheses into the combination family.
- The proposed body architecture is local to each hypothesis, not a decision about the eventual master. Quadruped and upright options both remain open. Architecture is a recorded variable and a possible confound; this is a breadth screen, not a factorial causal experiment.
- No alternate-architecture reruns, third samples, replacements, or additional views are included in the proposed 20-image budget. Further experiments require a later human decision.

### Accessory Compatibility / Dressing Architecture - Hard Constraint

All M01-M10 hypotheses must offer a plausible repeatable way to dress the same base architecture. Morphological abnormality remains desirable. Do not introduce human anatomy, standard clothing proportions, a conventional waist, or a generic humanoid body to make accessories easier. Custom CAPICAPI accessory geometry is expected; reuse means a coherent set of coverage and integration rules, not identical parts fitting every candidate or every role unchanged.

An Accessory Envelope is a conceptual usable spatial/structural zone around the head, face, neck transition, torso, back, limbs, or feet where future variant geometry can be added without fighting the base, losing its identity, or undermining FDM production. It includes coverage space, adjacent-zone conflicts, broad fusion surfaces, and areas that should remain visible. It is not a detachable socket system, physical dressing clearance, new anatomy, or geometry to draw on the naked candidate. No numerical dimensions, clearances, tolerances, minimum features, or mounting standards are approved in Round 1.

| Envelope | Future categories to consider conceptually | Compatibility and identity checks |
|---|---|---|
| Head / crown | Hats, helmets, caps, other headwear | Can custom headwear follow the skull with robust integration, ear accommodation, and a surviving crown/face relationship? Avoid thin brims and needing to shrink or relocate the skull. |
| Face | Glasses, goggles, masks, facial accessories | Are upper muzzle and lateral face surfaces usable without spanning unsupported empty space, thin glasses arms, hiding every capybara cue, or erasing the hook? |
| Neck transition | Scarves, collars, necklaces | Is there a readable transition or shoulder-level route for fused relief or volume, even without a human neck? Avoid choking off the face or requiring dangling loops/chains. |
| Torso / sides | Shirts, jackets, vests, uniforms, armor | Can fitted integrated coverage follow major body masses and forelimb roots without replacing the body architecture or filling every defining contour? |
| Back / rear | Capes, backpacks, rear-mounted accessories | Is broad fusion/contact possible with the body silhouette still legible? Avoid floating packs, suspended capes, bridging cavities, or accessory bulk swallowing the entire rear hook. |
| Waist, where applicable | Belts and waist elements | Can a morphology-specific torso band or relief route work without carving a waist? Anatomical absence may justify not applicable for a literal waist belt, but must not excuse broader torso restrictions. |
| Limbs / paws | Sleeves, wrist/arm elements, held props | Can short animal limbs carry integrated cuffs or fused/contact-supported props without adding human hands, finger grips, long arms, thin stems, or delicate sockets? |
| Feet / ground contact | Footwear, paw-mounted elements where appropriate | Can a conforming addition preserve the distinctive footprint, support contact, and stance without requiring long legs or unsupported soles? |
| Combined envelopes | Larger role-defining geometry; substantial head, torso, and rear coverage | Can adjacent categories coexist without mutually excluding routine combinations or obscuring all identity cues? A pack/headwear or torso/neck element conflict matters even when either fits alone. |

Evaluate headwear with torso coverage, face with neck-area coverage, and torso with rear-mounted volume as the same conceptual coexistence questions across all families. These are written spatial checks only, not role briefs, costume designs, rendered overlays, or image-generation instructions. Not every category must fit every role, and a justified local limitation is not a platform failure. Systematic exclusion of common categories/combinations, dependence on rebuilding major base masses, or recurrent manufacturing conflicts is a platform concern.

Record each applicable envelope as PLAUSIBLE, CONSTRAINED, DEAD_END, or UNKNOWN, plus supporting reasoning, a morphology-specific adaptation route, neighboring-zone conflicts, identity coverage risk, and apparent FDM risk. Use NOT_APPLICABLE only with a reason tied to anatomy or the category, never to hide a conflict. PLAUSIBLE remains conceptual, not validated fit. Do not invent a printable solution where the single neutral view provides no evidence.

A severe conflict lowers the relevant accessory/dressing/identity score and must be recorded as a hard-constraint concern; a high attractiveness or novelty score cannot compensate. A DEAD_END at platform level means repeated common coverage would require rebuilding the base, deleting its hook, adding fragile/support-dependent geometry, or relying on unworkable color architecture. Such a candidate cannot be recommended for advancement until the concern is resolved and human-reviewed. UNKNOWN is not a pass or automatic rejection. Preserve every candidate as evidence; humans remain authoritative and no score automatically selects or deletes anything.

### Naked Identity And Costumed Identity Survival

Record two separate assessments for every future candidate:

1. NAKED IDENTITY: does the neutral uncovered form retain both capybara recognition and its verbally describable signature mass relationship?
2. COSTUMED IDENTITY SURVIVAL: under plausible substantial coverage, which signature contours/mass relationships remain exposed or remain legible through conforming coverage? Which capybara cues survive, and which coverage would hide them?

Reason separately about substantial head coverage, torso coverage, rear coverage, and their plausible combinations. A custom garment that follows a distinctive belly or a shell that preserves a distinctive crown relationship may transmit the hook even when the surface is covered. A generic exterior that replaces that relationship, tiny visible patches, a logo, selected color, or an invented new feature do not count as identity survival. Do not rely exclusively on every future role leaving the single signature feature naked. Full enclosure need not preserve every cue, but routine substantial coverage must have a credible repeatable identity strategy. This is a conceptual review only; actual costume identity remains UNTESTED until separately authorized later tests.

### FDM Interaction

Accessory compatibility inherits `docs/product/FDM_DESIGN_STANDARD.md`, `docs/product/COLOR_DFM_STANDARD.md`, and the structural/relief/micro-detail rules in `docs/product/3D_MODELING_BIBLE.md`. Future dressing is integrated variant geometry, not a requirement for detachable clothes or manual assembly. Assess broad fused roots, continuous supported contact, and self-supporting transitions in a plausible intended build orientation. Relief can communicate collars, seams, cuffs, and bands without extra colors; micro-detail requires physical evidence later.

Headwear, masks, capes, packs, and held props are not compatible merely because space exists: repeated severe overhangs, long bridges, thin connectors, fragile appendages, or required supports/assembly remain conflicts. A base is also restrictive if recurring accessory layouts force fragmented color islands and excessive vertical overlap/swaps rather than plausible efficient Color-Z regions. Keep the existing maximum-four/target-three automatic-color policy as future context, without choosing colors now. Purge, minimum features, support-free feasibility, and strength remain TBD until geometry, slicer data, and physical tests; no production claim is made from an image.

### Fair Comparison Controls

Use one front-3/4 hero image per call, the same square canvas, same model/version and exposed quality settings, same approximate camera azimuth and modest elevation, same plain light neutral-grey background, same diffuse lighting, and uniform matte medium neutral-grey prototype material across every visible surface, including eyes and nose. No floor horizon, pedestal, dramatic shadows, glossy eyes, dark nose paint, faceted styling, fur, or decorative texture.

Center the full character and fit its longest projected dimension to approximately the same canvas span, leaving a clear border. Do not force equal character height: that would unfairly shrink the distinguishing horizontal extent of M07/M08. Canvas and camera controls are presentation constraints, not approved physical sizes or character ratios. Do not stretch or warp images to normalize framing.

Keep the shared capybara recognition cues constant in intent: a blunt broad projecting snout, small rounded high-set ears, small widely spaced eyes, sturdy animal paws, and no visible long tail. A family may distort a mass but must preserve enough of these cues to read immediately as capybara-derived. Use restrained smooth mass construction for every family so surface styling cannot substitute for architecture.

Neutral pose means a relaxed, stable pose appropriate to the proposed body architecture. Upright forms retain animal-derived anatomy, with short forepaws close to the torso; they do not acquire a human chest, waist, hands, or legs. Quadrupeds stand quietly on sturdy paws. Pose energy and facial emotion are not variables in this round.

Use no Round 0 candidate as a mandatory visual template for this breadth experiment. Later reference development still requires the specifically approved hero as image context. Do not attach an external collectible as a design target. Record model/version, settings and seed if exposed; mark unavailable fields unknown rather than inventing reproducibility. Keep both samples' prompts identical. A missing hypothesis or constraint violation is a generation-validity issue, not evidence that the hypothesis itself failed.

## Visual Reference Policy
